Tapping the bakery

  • Chunky round buttons with thick outlines and bright colors. Everything bounces and sparkles a little when you touch it.
  • Bubbles over buildings: a problem (bakery needs flour), a wish (houses want a park), or a reward you tap to collect (finished goal at the town hall).
  • Lena and the other key staff greet you and tell you what happened, one line at a time, with "Show me".
  • Goals sit as small icons on the left; finishing one pops a reward.
  • Tapping a building opens a big friendly card: what it makes as pictures, workers as little heads, one clear problem with a Fix button.

Trade-off: the most "game" feeling and the easiest to learn. It can get busy, and it needs care so it doesn't look like a pay-to-win mobile game.

Tapping the bakery

  • Almost no HUD: your coins, and a sun arc that shows the time of day. The island fills the screen and the camera sits a bit further back.
  • Small quiet markers appear only where something needs you. No outlines, no bounce.
  • What happened while you were away arrives as a handwritten postcard from Lena.
  • Tapping a building dims the rest and opens a ring of buttons around it, right in the world (upgrade, workers, details, close). A dotted line points to the cause: the windmill.

Trade-off: the most beautiful and relaxing. Deep management (production chains, staff, trade) is harder to fit, because more is hidden and found by tapping.

Tapping the bakery

  • A slim dark HUD with your coins, workers and key goods. Your key staff sit on the left; a red dot means one has news.
  • "Views" on the right recolor the island by topic. Here "Goods flow" draws moving lines from field to windmill to bakery to market, so you see the chain and exactly where it breaks.
  • Every producer wears a small icon of what it makes.
  • Tapping a building shows its whole production chain with a one-tap fix, its workers, and the staff rules that touch it.

Trade-off: shows the depth best and suits deep management. It puts the most on screen, so on a small phone it can start to feel like work.
